=====================================================================
  ABRITÉ: A HEALTH INSURANCE COMPARISON WEBSITE (MERN STACK)
=====================================================================


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. WHAT IS THIS PROJECT?
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Abrité is a health insurance comparison website that compares health
insurance plans offered by different banks in India and recommends the
ones that best suit a family.

 The problem
   Choosing health insurance is confusing. There are dozens of plans,
   each with fine print like waiting periods, room rent limits and
   co-payments, and most websites push whichever product they want to
   sell.

 The solution
   The user answers 14 short questions about their family, health,
   budget and preferences. The website then:
     - calculates how much cover (sum insured) the family needs,
     - removes plans the family cannot buy,
     - estimates the monthly and yearly price of every other plan,
     - ranks the plans with a match score (0–100%),
     - explains each plan's good and bad points in plain language.

 Key facts
   - Partner banks (8): HDFC Bank, State Bank of India, ICICI Bank,
     Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Yes Bank, Bank of Baroda and
     Punjab National Bank.
   - Plans compared: 16 (2 per bank).
   - Questions: 14, each with a follow-up question.
   - Accounts: users register and log in to save their results,
     shortlist plans ("I'm interested") and compare up to 3 plans side
     by side.
   - Built with the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express, React, Node.js); the
     data is stored in MongoDB Atlas, in the cloud.
   - Works on desktop, tablet and mobile.
   - The name "Abrité" is French for "sheltered" or "protected". It is
     an invented name, not a real company.
   - The bank names are real, but the plan names, prices and figures
     are sample data made for this project. They are not real offers.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. WHAT CAN A USER DO ON THE SITE?
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 - Read the home page: why health insurance matters, partner banks,
   FAQs.
 - Click "Get quotes" or "View plans" to open a pop-up questionnaire.
 - Answer 14 questions. Each one has a small FOLLOW-UP question that
   appears under it. For example, if you say "Yes" to tobacco, it asks
   "How often?". If you say "No", it asks about alcohol instead.
 - See a SUMMARY of every answer before submitting, with an Edit
   button next to each one.
 - Register or log in (the site asks only at this point, because your
   results are saved to your account).
 - See RESULTS: the recommended cover amount, then plan cards with a
   match %, monthly price, key features, and "Why this plan?".
 - Tick up to 3 plans and COMPARE them side by side in a table.
 - Click "I'm interested" on any plan to save it to a personal list.
 - Visit "My account" to see saved plans and past recommendations.
 - Browse "All plans" without answering anything.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. THE TECHNOLOGY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

MERN is just four tools that work well together. Think of a restaurant:

  M - MongoDB     The STORE ROOM. It keeps all the data: banks, plans,
                  users, saved results. We use the free cloud version,
                  "MongoDB Atlas", so the data lives online, not on
                  one laptop.

  E - Express     The KITCHEN. It receives requests ("give me the
                  plans", "log me in"), does the work, and sends back
                  an answer. It runs on Node.js.

  R - React       The DINING ROOM. Everything the customer sees and
                  clicks: pages, buttons, the pop-up, the cards.

  N - Node.js     The BUILDING the kitchen runs in. It lets us run
                  JavaScript on a computer or server, not just in a
                  browser.

So: the user clicks something in React → React asks Express → Express
fetches or saves data in MongoDB → Express replies → React shows the
result.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. THE USER'S JOURNEY, STEP BY STEP
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Let's follow one user, Hujaifa, through the whole site.

 Step 1, HOME PAGE
   Hujaifa lands on the home page. React asks the backend for the list
   of banks ("GET /api/banks") and shows 8 bank cards. On the right is
   a quote card, where Hujaifa picks a gender and ticks Self, spouse
   and Son.

 Step 2, THE POP-UP QUESTIONNAIRE
   Hujaifa clicks "View plans" and a pop-up (a "modal") opens. React
   asks the backend for the questions ("GET /api/questions").
   Interesting design choice: the questions live on the SERVER, not in
   the React code, so we can change a question in one place and both
   sides stay in sync.

   Hujaifa sees ONE question at a time. Once a question is answered, a
   follow-up box appears underneath. Answering the follow-up moves the
   pop-up to the next question by itself. The answers are kept in the
   browser's "sessionStorage", so refreshing the page doesn't lose
   them.

   The 14 questions are:
      1. Gender               → follow-up: occupation
      2. Who to insure        → do all live in the same city?
      3. Ages                 → medical check-up in the last year?
      4. City                 → which hospitals do you prefer?
      5. Existing illness     → since when? (or family history if none)
      6. Tobacco              → how often? (or alcohol if no)
      7. Hospital admissions  → reason? (or planned treatment if none)
      8. Income               → how many earning members?
      9. Monthly budget       → pay monthly / yearly / every 2–3 years?
     10. Employer cover       → covers parents? (or past policy if none)
     11. Room type            → want treatment at home covered?
     12. Co-pay               → what share? (or a deductible if no)
     13. Doctor visits (OPD)  → what for? (or free check-up if no)
     14. Top priority         → second priority?

 Step 3, THE SUMMARY
   After question 14, Hujaifa sees every answer listed, each with an
   Edit button. Editing one jumps the pop-up to that question, then it
   comes straight back to the summary.

 Step 4, REGISTER / LOG IN
   Hujaifa clicks "Confirm & view plans". Since Hujaifa isn't logged
   in, the pop-up shows a register form. (We ask only now, at the
   moment it's actually needed, which is friendlier.) Hujaifa creates
   an account.

 Step 5, THE MOMENT
   React sends all the answers to the backend ("POST /api/assessments").
   The backend:
     - double-checks every answer is valid,
     - loads all 16 plans from MongoDB,
     - runs them through the "engine" (explained in section 7),
     - saves the result in the database,
     - replies with an ID number for this result.
   React then opens the results page for that ID.

 Step 6, RESULTS
   Hujaifa sees "We recommend a cover of ₹15 Lakh" (with an explanation
   of how we got that number), then the plan cards. The top one has a
   "Best match for you" ribbon. Each card shows the match %, monthly
   price, a feature list, an "I'm interested" button, and "Why this
   plan?", which opens the reasons and drawbacks. Plans the family
   can't buy are listed at the bottom with the reason, e.g. "Entry
   closes at age 40".

 Step 7, COMPARE AND SAVE
   Hujaifa ticks 3 plans → "Compare plans" → a table with a ★ on the
   best value in each row. Clicking "I'm interested" on two plans puts
   them in "My account", along with all past recommendations.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. THE BACKEND, FILE BY FILE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 index.js, THE RECEPTION
   The first file that runs. It loads the secret settings from .env,
   connects to the database, makes sure the banks and plans are there,
   and starts listening for requests on port 5050. If the database
   can't be reached, it prints a clear message saying what to check,
   instead of a scary error.

 db.js
   Connects to MongoDB Atlas. It has a small trick: some Windows
   networks fail to look up Atlas's address, so if that happens it
   retries using Google's public address book (DNS). It also has
   seedCatalogue(), which fills the database with banks and plans from
   data.js if they're missing.

 data.js
   The list of 8 banks and 16 plans (2 per bank). Each plan has its
   rules: room rent limit, waiting period for existing illnesses,
   co-pay, OPD cover, maximum entry age, base price, and so on.
   Important: the BANK NAMES are real, but the plan names and numbers
   are SAMPLE data we made up for learning. They are not real offers.

 models.js
   Defines the shape of each kind of record we store. There are 5
   collections (a "collection" in MongoDB is like a table):
      banks        - the 8 banks
      plans        - the 16 plans, each linked to its bank
      users        - name, email, mobile, and a HASHED password
      assessments  - one saved questionnaire + its ranked results,
                     linked to a user
      interests    - plans a user marked "interested", plus the price
                     they saw, linked to the user and the plan

 questions.js
   Holds the 14 questions and their follow-ups. It also has
   validateAnswers(), which checks every answer the browser sends
   BEFORE we use it. Never trust data from the browser blindly;
   someone could send rubbish on purpose. The server always checks.

 engine.js
   Takes the answers and the plans, and returns the ranked list
   (explained in section 7).

 auth.js
   Handles register and login, and has a guard called requireAuth that
   stands in front of private pages and checks your "pass" (token).

 routes.js
   The list of every address the frontend can call and which file
   handles it. For example:
      POST /api/auth/register   → create an account
      POST /api/auth/login      → log in
      GET  /api/questions       → the questionnaire
      GET  /api/banks           → the banks
      GET  /api/plans           → the plans
      POST /api/assessments     → submit answers, get ranked plans
      GET  /api/assessments     → my past results
      GET/POST/DELETE /api/interests → my interested plans

   GET means "give me something", POST "create something", DELETE
   "remove something".

 seed.js and engine.test.js, THE HELPERS
   seed.js reloads the catalogue when you run "npm run seed".
   engine.test.js holds 8 automatic tests ("npm test") that check the
   brain still behaves correctly after any change.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. HOW LOGIN AND SECURITY WORK
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 Storing passwords
   We never store the actual password. It goes through "bcrypt", which
   turns "mypassword123" into something like "$2a$10$Xk9...". This is
   called hashing, and it's one-way: you can't turn the hash back into
   the password. When you log in, bcrypt checks whether what you typed
   matches the stored hash. So even if someone stole our database,
   they wouldn't get anyone's password.

 Staying logged in: the JWT "wristband"
   Think of a concert. At the gate you show your ticket once (email +
   password), and they give you a wristband. After that you just show
   the wristband.

   Our wristband is a "JWT" (JSON Web Token). After login, the server
   gives the browser a token that says "this is user number 6b07…",
   signed with a secret key only the server knows (JWT_SECRET in .env).
   Nobody can forge or edit it without that key. It expires after 7
   days.

   The browser keeps the token in "localStorage" and attaches it to
   every request as:   Authorization: Bearer <token>

   On private routes, requireAuth checks the token. Valid → the request
   goes through, and we know WHO it is. Missing or invalid → the server
   replies "401 Please log in to continue".

   Logging out simply deletes the token from the browser.

 Privacy
   When you ask for a saved result, the server looks it up by ID AND
   your user ID, so you can only ever see your own results, never
   someone else's.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 7. THE BRAIN (engine.js)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 Example family: Hujaifa (34), spouse (31) and their son. They live in
 Mumbai, have no illness, don't smoke, and want to spend about ₹2,000
 a month.

 Step 1, MAKE A PROFILE
   Turn the answers into simple facts: 2 adults, 1 child, 3 people in
   total, eldest age 34, metro city, no illness, budget ₹2,000.

 Step 2, HOW MUCH COVER DO THEY NEED?
   Start from the city: metros are expensive, so ₹10 lakh (large
   cities ₹7 lakh, others ₹5 lakh). Each extra adult adds 40% and each
   child 15%:
        10 × (1 + 0.40 + 0.15) = 15.5 lakh
   Add more if someone is 45+ or 60+, or has an illness. Subtract half
   of any office cover (because it disappears if you change jobs).
   Don't exceed what their income can sensibly support. Then round to
   the nearest standard amount: ₹15 lakh.

 Step 3, REMOVE PLANS THEY CAN'T BUY
   A plan is removed if the eldest person is too old for it, if it's a
   senior-citizen plan but the family is young, if it covers only one
   person, or if it refuses an illness someone has. These are shown
   at the bottom of the results, with the reason.

 Step 4, ESTIMATE THE PRICE
   Every plan has a "base rate": the yearly price for ONE 30-year-old
   with ₹5 lakh cover in a metro. We adjust it:
        × age factor      (older is more expensive; age 34 → ×1)
        × family factor   (3 people → ×1.5, not ×3)
        × cover factor    (₹15L is about ×1.64 the ₹5L price)
        × city factor     (metro ×1, large city ×0.9, others ×0.8)
        × risk loading    (daily tobacco +15%, each illness +7%,
                           a co-pay makes it cheaper)
        × 1.18            (18% GST)
   For HDFC's "Optimum Family Health" (base ₹6,500), that comes to
   about ₹18,862 a year, or about ₹1,572 a month.

 Step 5, GIVE A MATCH SCORE (0–100%)
   We score each plan from 0 to 1 on several things:
      - Price:    how it compares to the budget (cheaper is better;
                  over budget drops fast)
      - Coverage: room rent freedom, restore benefit, bonus, etc.
      - Claims:   how many claims the bank pays, and how fast
      - Network:  how many cashless hospitals
      - Room:     does the room rule match what they asked for?
      - Co-pay:   do they have to share the bill?
   Plus extra items ONLY if relevant: illness waiting time, OPD,
   restore after a claim, a free check-up.

   Then each item gets a WEIGHT (how much it matters), and the user's
   top priority counts DOUBLE. If Hujaifa chose "Lowest premium", the
   price would matter twice as much as for someone who chose "Widest
   coverage". The second priority counts 1.4×.

   The final match % is the weighted average. Simple idea, powerful
   result: two families can see the same plans in a completely
   different order, because they care about different things.

 Step 6, EXPLAIN IN PLAIN ENGLISH
   For every plan we write short reasons ("Within your budget at
   ₹1,572 a month", "No room rent limit") and drawbacks ("You pay 20%
   of every claim"). We list the things the user specifically asked
   about first, because those matter most to them.

 Finally, we sort by match %, cheapest first if two plans tie.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 8. THE FRONTEND
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 - main.jsx starts React and puts the App on the page.
 - App.jsx is the frame of every page: the header (logo, menu, Log in /
   My account), the footer, and the "routes", which say which page to
   show for which address (/ → Home, /plans/123 → Results, /compare →
   Compare, and so on). Pages like Results and My account are wrapped
   in a "Private" guard that sends logged-out users to the login page.
 - lib/api.js is the ONLY place that talks to the backend. Every page
   calls api.something(), and this file adds the token automatically.
 - lib/session.jsx remembers who is logged in and their interested
   plans, and shares that with every page (React "Context").
 - components/Wizard.jsx is the pop-up: it shows one question at a
   time, the follow-up, the summary, and the login step.
 - pages/ has one file per page, each doing its own small job.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 9. ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY (FOR THE END OF A PRESENTATION)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

 Abrité is a MERN-stack website that helps a family choose health
 insurance. A pop-up asks 14 simple questions, each with a follow-up.
 After a quick account sign-up, a server-side engine works out how
 much cover the family needs, removes plans they can't buy, estimates
 each plan's price, and scores every plan from 0 to 100 based on what
 the family said matters most. Users see clear reasons and drawbacks,
 can compare plans side by side, and can save the plans they like.
 React builds the screens, Express runs the logic and security
 (bcrypt passwords, JWT tokens), and MongoDB Atlas stores the banks,
 plans, users, results and saved plans.
